🏛️ Chapter I: The Anatomy of a S$1.18M HDB Transaction

In February 2017, a 120-square-meter 5-room flat on an elevated storey in Bishan’s Natura Loft transacted for S$1,180,000. At first glance, a seven-figure sum for government-subsidized public housing appears paradoxical. Yet, our empirical decomposition reveals an exact mathematical formula behind this record: an unexpired 93.6-year leasehold, premier DBSS condominium-spec architecture, an elevated storey range, and location in a top-tier school district.

Across all 5,000 transactions in this curated dataset, public housing has split into two divergent economies: an affordability-focused outer ring providing essential shelter volume, and a speculative prime cluster where buyers gladly pay upwards of S$9,700 per square meter to secure central accessibility and fresh lease longevity.

⏳ The 99-Year Clock: Unmasking Simpson's Paradox

Why raw aggregated data creates a counter-intuitive U-shaped lease curve, and how controlling for estate maturity reveals the true steepness of depreciation.

💡 Statistical Insight: Raw island-wide data shows flats with <60 years lease priced at ~S$4,800/sqm, higher than 75-year-old flats (~S$3,850/sqm). This is a classic Simpson's Paradox: the oldest flats are concentrated exclusively in prime city-fringe pioneer towns (Queenstown, Marine Parade, Central Area), while mid-aged flats are in outer non-mature towns. Once isolated by estate type (Facebook Blue vs Green curves), mature estate lease decay is severe and steep.

📊 Empirical Findings Dossier

1. The +S$139,694 Mature Estate Hedonic Arbitrage

Location in a mature estate commands an isolated S$139,694 premium (or S$172,626 for 4-Room flats specifically). Mature towns trade at a 22.6% price-per-sqm premium despite flats being an average of 9.0 years older (68.9 yrs vs 77.9 yrs remaining lease). Proximity to core transit lines and prestige primary schools consistently out-bids physical age decay.

2. The Million-Dollar Sieve (Fresh Lease + Prestige Code)

All 9 million-dollar flats in Q1 2017 occurred in mature estates, with 88.9% retaining >92 years remaining lease. Ordinary public housing rarely crosses the million-dollar threshold unless paired with specialized architectural typologies: Type S1/S2 units at Pinnacle@Duxton (median S$9,485/sqm) and DBSS developments like Natura Loft (median S$7,644/sqm) trade at a +75% to +120% premium over standard blocks.

3. Vertical Storey Elevation Dividend (+S$6,670/Floor)

Our multivariate model proves that buyers pay an empirical premium of S$6,670 for every additional storey of elevation. High-floor units (Storeys 25–40) in Queenstown and Central Area command S$100,000–S$180,000 more than identical low-floor ground units in the same block, insulating them against localized price softening.

🎯 Actionable Executive Playbook

1. First-Time Buyer Play: The Sengkang/Punggol Capital Moat

First-time buyers with combined household incomes of S$8,000–S$12,000 should bypass mature estate premiums. Allocating S$420k–S$480k into 4-Room or 5-Room flats in Punggol (mean lease 91.2 yrs) or Sengkang (87.3 yrs) provides 95–118 sqm of functional living space while guaranteeing over 65 years of remaining leasehold upon exit, preserving resale equity for subsequent private upgrading.

2. Upgrader Warning: The Mature "<60-Year Liquidity Trap"

Older mature flats (<60 years lease in Ang Mo Kio, Bedok, Toa Payoh) appear superficially affordable at ~S$330k–S$380k, but carry accelerating structural risk. Secondary buyers face severe CPF withdrawal limits and shortened bank loan tenures when lease drops below 60 years. Upgraders holding such assets face compressed liquidity windows and should reallocate toward young mature resale stock.

3. Institutional Underwriting Benchmark (S$3,264 / Yr Rule)

Valuation models, mortgage insurers, and proptech platforms should adopt our hedonic parameter: S$3,264 per remaining lease year and S$3,799 per square meter (R² = 0.724). This establishes an objective standard to decouple intrinsic land/amenity value from leasehold expiration, identifying arbitrage mispricings across secondary market listings.
